Engineering biology

What it is

Biology answers why a system ended up in state X. The engineering question is different. Which intervention moves the system from state X to a required X*, and what happens after it.

We do not create a new science and do not claim superiority of the approach. The project shows that an engineering layer is needed in addition to descriptive biology. A model must produce a quantitative prediction before the experiment, and the disagreement between computation and measurement measures the model's quality. A negative result is kept and refines the model.

Method

Engineering cycle

Seven steps that repeat until the disagreement between computation and measurement is acceptable for a given task.

  1. Collect data. Determine which parameters of the living system can be measured and how reproducibly they hold.
  2. Build a model. Describe the relation between the state and external interventions formally, with explicit assumptions.
  3. State a prediction. Fix, before the experiment, the quantitative result the model considers expected and the bounds within which it holds.
  4. Perform the intervention. Carry out the intervention under controlled conditions, with a control group, so that it can be repeated.
  5. Measure the result. Record the result with the same methods used for the initial state so that the comparison is valid.
  6. Compare computation and experiment. Estimate the magnitude and sign of the prediction error. A negative result is as much a result as a positive one.
  7. Update the model. Change the model in the light of the disagreement and return to step 3 with a new prediction.

Progress is measured by reducing the error between prediction and measurement, not by the number of publications or the persuasiveness of an explanation.

Organisation goals

What it builds on

Engineering biology builds on established fields and does not set itself against them.

Systems biology
Describing interacting biological systems and their dynamics.
Synthetic biology
Designing biological constructions and deliberately altering systems.
Computational biology
Analysing biological data from which a measurable state is built.
Machine learning
Building models and assessing their predictions outside training conditions.
Control theory
The language of state, control and feedback in which an intervention is formulated.
Experimental biology
Testing models in living organisms and cell cultures.

About

Center for Engineering Biology

The project develops an engineering approach to living systems and publishes experiment records as they are completed.

Engineering biology here does not replace synthetic or computational biology; it adds a requirement for the full cycle. Project texts are published on Habr.
